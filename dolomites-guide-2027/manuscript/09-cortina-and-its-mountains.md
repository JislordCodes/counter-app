# 9. Cortina d'Ampezzo and Its Mountains

Cortina d'Ampezzo is the most famous town in the Dolomites. It hosted the 1956 Winter Olympics and, together with Milan, the 2026 Winter Games. It sits in a wide bowl of peaks and is the best base for the Cinque Torri, the Lagazuoi tunnels, the Passo Giau and Lago di Sorapis.

It is also a real town, with shops, restaurants and a busy centre. That makes it different from the quiet villages of Val Gardena and Alta Badia, and it comes with its own costs and rules.

## Overview

| | |
|---|---|
| **Province** | Veneto (Belluno) |
| **Main base** | Cortina d'Ampezzo; smaller bases at Passo Falzarego, Pocol and Misurina |
| **Language** | Italian, with Ladin spoken locally |
| **Best for** | Great War history, big scenery, mountain huts, and visitors who like a proper town |
| **Car needed?** | Helpful. There is no railway. |
| **Nearest airports** | Venice (about 2 to 2.5 hours), Treviso (about 2 hours) |
| **Season** | Summer lifts from late May or June; winter from December |

## Getting there and around

- **From Venice airport.** The Cortina Express bus runs from Marco Polo airport to the Cortina bus station. In 2026 the trip took about 2 hours 10 minutes to under 2.5 hours, with fares around €21 one way. Check the current price.
- **By car.** From Venice, take the motorway to Belluno and then the main road north (SS51). From Val Pusteria, drive from Dobbiaco to Cortina.
- **Within the area.** In summer, the Dolomiti Bus network serves the passes, including the Passo Giau. In winter, a ski bus service links the town with the slopes.
- **Town centre.** Cortina has a **ZTL** (limited traffic zone) system, with a heavily restricted pedestrian area around Corso Italia. Do not drive into it by accident. Ask your hotel how to reach it by car.
- **Lifts.** The Dolomiti SuperSummer card covers many of Cortina's lifts (Chapter 4).

> **Wish I'd Known:** Cortina is the Dolomites base with the least reliable car-free options. Without a car, you rely on buses that run to the passes on fixed times. Check the last bus back before you set out.

## What changed in 2026

After the Winter Olympics in February 2026, Cortina had a valley full of new or upgraded roads, lifts and town infrastructure, and summer 2026 was the first season to see how it worked. One major project, the Apollonio–Socrepes cable car, was not ready for the Games, and prosecutors opened an investigation into it in May 2026. Check whether it is open before you plan to use it.

**What it means for you.** Expect building work and some changes in 2027, and confirm lift openings with the local operators.

## 1. Cortina town

| At a glance | |
|---|---|

## 2. Cinque Torri

| At a glance | |
|---|---|
| **Overview** | Five rock towers with an open-air Great War museum around them: reconstructed trenches, artillery positions and bunkers. |
| **Why visit** | History and views, with little walking if you take the chairlift. A good choice for families. |
| **Location and access** | Chairlift from the Bai de Dones area (near the Passo Falzarego road). The lift opened on 6 June in 2026. |
| **Cost (2026)** | Chairlift about €15. The open-air museum is free. Parking about €5 to €10 a day; bring cash because card machines may fail. |
| **Visit time** | Half a day. |
| **Best time** | Early morning in summer. Early September has clearer air and fewer visitors. |
| **Crowd reality** | Busy at midday. Avoid mid-August (Ferragosto). |
| **Difficulty** | Easy around the towers. Steeper to Rifugio Nuvolau. |
| **Practical tips** | Take layers. The upper station has Rifugio Scoiattoli, with a terrace and a children's playground. |
| **Common mistakes** | Arriving late. Not carrying cash for parking. |
| **Nearby** | Passo Falzarego, Lagazuoi, Passo Giau. |
| **Drawbacks** | Popular. The walk to Nuvolau is short but steep. |

## 3. Lagazuoi and the Great War tunnels

| At a glance | |
|---|---|
| **Overview** | A mountain at about 2,800 m with tunnels cut by Italian troops through the rock in 1915 to 1917. The Galleria del Lagazuoi is a walkable route through them. |
| **Why visit** | One of the best places in the Dolomites to understand the Great War in the mountains. |
| **Location and access** | Cable car from Passo Falzarego (about 2,105 m). The lift opened on 30 May in 2026. |
| **Cost (2026)** | Cable car about €29.50 (return). Helmet and headtorch are required; rental was about €5. |
| **Visit time** | The tunnel walk takes nearly 3 hours. A full loop is about 10 km and at least half a day. |
| **Best time** | Summer and early autumn. Go early. |
| **Crowd reality** | Busy at midday. |
| **Difficulty** | For fit adults. The tunnels are dark, wet and slippery, with iron and stone steps. Not suitable for children under 8 or for dogs. |
| **Practical tips** | Bring warm layers: the tunnels are cold and damp even in summer. Use a headtorch with spare batteries. |
| **Common mistakes** | Wearing trainers. Starting late. Skipping the helmet. |
| **Nearby** | Cinque Torri, Passo Valparola, Alta Badia by bus. |
| **Drawbacks** | Not for everyone: dark, steep and wet. |

## 4. Passo Giau

| At a glance | |
|---|---|
| **Overview** | A high pass road at about 2,236 m with wide meadow views and a long list of switchbacks. |
| **Why visit** | A scenic drive or ride, and a start point for hikes toward Lago Federa and Croda da Lago. |
| **Access** | By car, bike or the Dolomiti Bus summer service. |
| **Parking** | Reports differ. One source describes free parking at the top and in a few wide spaces. Another says roadside parking at Col Gallina and the pass is prohibited, with towing and fines. Check the signs on arrival. |
| **Best time** | Early morning or late afternoon. |
| **Crowd reality** | Busy with cars, motorbikes and cyclists on summer weekends. |
| **Common mistakes** | Parking on the verge. Stopping on blind bends. |
| **Drawbacks** | Narrow road. Limited space. |

## 5. Lago di Sorapis

| At a glance | |
|---|---|
| **Overview** | A turquoise lake carved into the rock, with no surface outlet. The Rifugio Vandelli, built in 1890, stands nearby. |
| **Why visit** | The colour of the water, and a rewarding day walk. |
| **Location and access** | Starts at Passo Tre Croci, on route 215. |
| **Distance and time** | About 10.5 km round trip, about 4 hours, with about 434 m of climbing (Cortina tourist board). Other sources say 11 to 13 km and 4 to 5 hours. |
| **Difficulty** | The tourist board rates it "for experts". It has metal ladders, cables and exposed ledges. |
| **Support** | Rifugio Vandelli and Ristorante Son Zuogo. |
| **Best time** | Summer and early September, in dry weather. |
| **Crowd reality** | Very popular. Start early. |
| **Common mistakes** | Treating it as an easy walk. Wearing slippery shoes. Going in wet weather. |
| **Drawbacks** | Exposed sections; narrow ledges can cause queues. |

## 6. Faloria, Tofane and Cristallo lifts

- **Faloria.** A cable car above Cortina. A return ticket was reported at around €23 in 2026 (check current price). It opened in July in 2026.
- **Tofane.** A lift area west of town. In 2026 it opened on 13 June, with return tickets around €25 to €35 depending on the sections.
- **Cristallo.** A mountain group above the Tre Croci road, used for hikes and via ferrata (Chapter 14).

## Where to stay

Choose **Cortina** for restaurants, services and the most bus links. Choose **Passo Falzarego** for the earliest start at Lagazuoi. Consider **Misurina** or **Sesto** (Chapter 10) if Tre Cime is your main goal. See Chapter 6.

## Where to eat

Cortina has a wide range of restaurants and cafés. Mountain huts, such as Scoiattoli and Vandelli, serve lunch with views. Book dinners in July and August, and carry cash for huts.

## Photography notes

- Early morning on the Cinque Torri, before the crowd.
- Late afternoon on the Tofane and Cristallo from town and the passes.
- Lago di Sorapis is best in clear weather when the water colour is strongest.
- Drones need care in protected areas and around huts.

## Rainy-day options

Browse Cortina's shops and cafés, visit museums and galleries, and plan a spa session. If the cloud lifts, the Cinque Torri open-air museum is a gentle option.

## Common mistakes in this region

- Driving into the ZTL.
- Parking on Passo Giau verges.
- Doing Sorapis in wet weather.
- Wearing trainers in the Lagazuoi tunnels.
- Assuming Cortina has good car-free links to other valleys.

## A simple plan for three days

- **Day 1:** Cinque Torri early, then lunch at Rifugio Scoiattoli. Afternoon in Cortina.
- **Day 2:** Lagazuoi tunnels with a helmet and headtorch, plus a stop at Passo Falzarego.
- **Day 3:** Lago di Sorapis if the weather is dry; otherwise a drive over Passo Giau.
