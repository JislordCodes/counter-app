# 17. Culture, History, Etiquette and Photography

You do not need to know the history of the Dolomites to enjoy them. But a little background changes what you notice: why signs have three names, why war tunnels run through famous peaks, and why some viewpoints now have fences and fees.

This chapter explains what matters for your trip, how to behave on the trail and in huts, which rules carry fines, and how to take better photographs.

## Three languages, one region

South Tyrol passed from Austria to Italy in 1919, when the population was about 85 percent German-speaking. The Italian state tried to replace German with Italian, especially in the Fascist years (1922 to 1943). In 1972, a Second Autonomy Statute gave the province wide powers over its own affairs and protected its languages.

**What this means for you**
- Signs, menus and websites often appear in German and Italian, and in Ladin in some valleys. Place names differ (Chapter 1).
- In 2024, about 57.6 percent of South Tyrol's population used German as a first language, about 22.6 percent Italian and about 3.7 percent Ladin.
- Many staff in shops, hotels and huts speak some English. Starting with a greeting in the local language is appreciated.
- Public services and signs are bilingual by law, so you will see both names on the same sign.

### The Ladin people

Ladin is a Romance language that has been spoken in the mountains for more than a thousand years. It is spoken in five areas: Val Gardena, Val Badia, Val di Fassa, Livinallongo and Cortina d'Ampezzo. One estimate puts speakers in Val Badia and Val Gardena at about 18,000.

- **See it:** the Museum Ladin Ciastel de Tor in San Martino in Badia (Chapter 8), and the Museum Gherdëina in Ortisei (Chapter 7).
- **Taste it:** Ladin dishes such as tutres, cajincí and panicia (Chapter 16).
- **Hear it:** you will often hear Ladin in the villages of Val Gardena and Alta Badia.

## The Great War in the mountains

From 1915 to 1917, the Italian and Austro-Hungarian armies fought along the Dolomites. Soldiers lived at over 2,500 metres in winter. They dug tunnels, built gun positions in the rock and fought in places that are now ordinary walking routes.

**Why it matters for your trip.** Many of the best-known routes follow war paths, and some are now open-air museums.

| Site | What you see | Chapter |
|---|---|---|
| **Cortina Great War Open-Air Museums** | Lagazuoi, Cinque Torri, Sass di Stria and Forte Tre Sassi, within about 5 km of each other | 9 |
| **Lagazuoi tunnels** | Tunnels cut by Italian troops in 1915 to 1917 | 9 |
| **Cinque Torri** | Trenches, gun positions and bunkers; free to enter | 9 |
| **Marmolada, Serauta station** | Great War museum, included with the cable car ticket | 11 |
| **Passo Pordoi museum** | Opened in 2018; more than 3,000 finds. Entry €6, reduced €4, family €12, children under 10 free (reported). Bus stop June to September | 8 |
| **Monte Piana** | Open-air museum near Misurina, with a short theme path | 10 |
| **Tre Cime tunnels** | Short tunnel detour near Rifugio Locatelli | 10 |

**How to behave.** Treat these places with respect. Soldiers died here. Do not take objects, do not climb on trench walls, and stay on marked paths.

## Mountains and mountaineers

- **Messner Mountain Museum.** Reinhold Messner, born in Brixen in 1944, was the first person to climb all 14 peaks above 8,000 metres. His project has six museums: Firmian (Sigmundskron Castle, near Bolzano), Juval (in the Vinschgau valley), Dolomites (Monte Rite, south of Cortina), Ortles (Sulden), Ripa (Bruneck Castle) and Corones (on Plan de Corones, Chapter 12). A multi-museum ticket exists.
- **Cortina and the Olympics.** Cortina hosted the Winter Olympics in 1956 and, with Milan, in 2026 (Chapter 9).
- **UNESCO.** Nine mountain groups were added to the World Heritage List in 2009 (Chapter 1).

## The legend of the pink mountains

Visitors often ask why the peaks turn pink at sunset. The Ladin word for this glow is **enrosadira**. It is a real light effect, caused by low sun on pale rock.

An old legend explains it differently. King Laurin of the dwarfs had a garden of roses on the Catinaccio mountain (the Rosengarten). When he was captured, he cursed the roses so that no one could see them by day or night. But he forgot the twilight, so at dusk the roses still glow.

## Etiquette: how to behave

### Greetings

- **Italian:** *Buongiorno* (good day) until the evening, then *Buonasera*.
- **German:** *Grüß Gott* is the common greeting in South Tyrol.
- **Informal:** *Ciao* works with fellow walkers.
- **On the trail:** A greeting or a smile to every walker you pass is the norm.

### On the trail

- **Uphill walkers have priority** on narrow paths. Step aside and let them keep their rhythm.
- **Share the path** with cyclists and e-bikes. Slow down.
- **Close gates** and give cattle space.
- **Keep dogs on a lead** near livestock.
- **Keep your voice low** near huts and in forests.

### In mountain huts

- **Take boots off** at the door and use the boot room. Bring indoor shoes or slippers.
- **Lights out and silence** from about 22:00 to 06:00. Some huts turn their generator off at 22:00. Keep your things tidy and pack quietly in the morning.
- **Respect shared rooms.** Use a headlamp, not the main light.
- **Pay for what you use.** Keep your receipt.

### In restaurants and bars

- Say hello when you walk in.
- Tipping and the *coperto* are explained in Chapter 16.
- Standing at the counter for coffee is normal.

## Responsible travel and rules that carry fines

| Rule | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **No drones in protected areas.** Since January 2023, flying drones under 25 kg has been banned in 18 protected areas of South Tyrol, including the Tre Cime, Puez-Odle (Seceda), Fanes-Sennes-Braies (Lago di Braies) and Sciliar-Catinaccio parks, and Lago di Carezza. Private, social-media and promotional filming is not approved. In Veneto, the Dolomiti d'Ampezzo park needs written permission. | Drones disturb wildlife and other visitors. Check the D-Flight app for no-fly zones. In Italy, drones also need registration, a QR code and liability insurance. |
| **Do not pick flowers, mushrooms, minerals or fossils** in protected areas. | Many plants are protected, including edelweiss, gentians, orchids and arnica. |
| **No camping in nature parks.** | Wild camping is not allowed in most areas. Use campsites or huts. |
| **Stay on marked paths.** | Protects meadows and prevents erosion. |
| **Respect private land.** | Seceda's ridge viewpoint now has a paid turnstile set up by landowners. Santa Maddalena has a barrier for day visitors. Locals have asked visitors to stay off farm meadows. |
| **Park only where allowed.** | Roadside parking at some passes can be fined or towed. |
| **Take litter home.** | Huts have limited ways to remove waste from the mountain. |

**Other ways to travel lightly**
- Use buses and lifts instead of driving where you can.
- Visit famous places at off-peak times.
- Buy local food and stay in locally owned places.

## Photography guide

### Light and timing

Sunrise and sunset times in the Dolomites change a lot through the year. These times are for Ortisei (flat horizon, local clock time) and are computed from the position of the sun. Mountains block the sun, so in a valley the sun appears later in the morning and sets earlier in the evening.

| Date (2027) | Sunrise | Sunset |
|---|---|---|
| 21 June | 05:19 | 21:09 |
| 15 July | 05:34 | 21:04 |
| 15 August | 06:10 | 20:25 |
| 15 September | 06:49 | 19:27 |
| 15 October | 07:29 | 18:28 |
| 21 December | 07:54 | 16:28 |

**Enrosadira (the pink glow).** It appears in the minutes before sunrise and after sunset. Be in place at least 30 to 45 minutes early, and stay until the light fades.

> **Wish I'd Known:** Most lifts run from about 08:30 to 17:30 (the Seceda lifts, for example, ran about 08:30 to 17:30 in 2026). That means you cannot ride up for sunrise or sunset. For those shots at lift-served viewpoints, you need to walk up in the dark, or sleep in a hut.

### Best viewpoints and when to go

| Place | Best light and notes | Crowds |
|---|---|---|
| **Seceda** | Opinions differ: one guide reports the best light at sunrise, with the sun behind the peaks; another says morning is backlit and afternoon sun lights the Odle. Walk beyond the lift station. | Very high midday |
| **Alpe di Siusi** | Sunrise or sunset, with rolling hills, wooden huts and distant peaks. | Low early and late |
| **Lago di Braies** | Early morning, with the boathouse in the frame. | Very high from mid-morning |
| **Santa Maddalena** | Late afternoon and sunset, within the access rules. | High; 600 people a day at peak |
| **Tre Cime** | Sunset or sunrise light from the loop; plan to be at the viewpoint 20 to 30 minutes before sunset. | Very high midday |
| **Cinque Torri** | Early morning before the crowd. | High midday |
| **Croda da Lago and Lago Federa** | Reflections on calm days. Check conditions on the morning. | Lower than the big names |
| **Catinaccio from Gardeccia or the Vajolet area** | Late light on the towers. | High midday |

### Seasonal opportunities

- **June to early July:** meadow flowers, long days, and fresh snow patches high up.
- **Late summer:** clear air, stable weather.
- **Autumn:** low warm light, gold larches (usually mid to late October at mid-level), and the sun's position is better for many viewpoints.
- **Winter:** snow-covered peaks and villages with lights.

### Weather and crowds

- **After rain,** the air is often at its clearest.
- **Storm clouds** can make dramatic pictures, but leave exposed places if thunder is near.
- **Fog** in valleys can create layered views from a high viewpoint.
- **To avoid crowds:** go early or late, walk 15 minutes beyond the first viewpoint, and visit in September or on weekdays.

### Practical tips

- Take a **tripod** for dawn and dusk, a **polarising filter** for lakes and a **headlamp** for the walk in the dark.
- Carry **spare batteries**. Cold drains them quickly, especially in winter and at altitude.
- Be considerate: do not block paths for a photo, and stay off private fields.
- Ask before photographing people.

## Common mistakes

- Planning a sunrise shoot at a lift-served viewpoint.
- Flying a drone in a protected area.
- Walking onto private meadows for a better angle.
- Picking flowers.
- Speaking loudly in a hut after 22:00.
