## How to Use This Guide

This book is built to help you make decisions. It is not a catalogue of pretty places.

**Start with Part 1.** It covers the things that most often go wrong: booking rules, transport, costs and choosing a base. Fifteen minutes here can save you hours and hundreds of euros.

**Then read your region chapters.** Each one has the same layout, so you can compare places quickly.

**At-a-Glance boxes.** Every major sight has a box with the facts you need: how to get there, how long it takes, what it costs, when to go, how busy it is, how hard it is, and what people get wrong.

**Wish I'd Known boxes.** These hold the details that visitors most often say they wish they had known before arriving.

**Itineraries.** Chapter 18 has day-by-day plans with times, drive times and backup plans. You can follow them as written or use them as a starting point.

**2026 prices.** Prices marked "2026" show the likely level in 2027. They are not promises.

**Check before you go.** Rules change quickly. Appendix C lists the official websites to check before you book or travel.

## The Dolomites at a Glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Where** | North-east Italy, across the provinces of South Tyrol (Bolzano), Trentino and Veneto (Belluno) |
| **Highest peak** | Marmolada, 3,343 m |
| **World Heritage** | UNESCO site since 2009 (nine mountain groups) |
| **Languages** | Italian, German and Ladin |
| **Currency** | Euro |
| **Walking season** | Roughly mid-June to late September; lifts and huts open and close on fixed dates |
| **Ski season** | Roughly early December to early April |
| **Nearest big airports** | Venice, Verona, Innsbruck, Munich (Bolzano has limited flights) |
| **Emergency number** | 112 |

**Five things to book first**

1. Your accommodation, especially for July, August, Christmas and February.
2. Your Seceda lift tickets, if Seceda is on your list.
3. Your Tre Cime toll road slot, once booking opens (about 30 days ahead in 2026).
4. Car access or parking for Lago di Braies, if you will drive there in July to mid-September.
5. Mountain huts, if you plan to sleep above the valleys.

Chapter 3 explains each one.

## 12 Things Most Visitors Wish They Knew

1. **Famous places now run on reservations.** In 2026, Seceda lifts, Lago di Braies by car, the Tre Cime toll road and the Alpe di Siusi road all had booking or timed-access rules in summer.
2. **Short distances are not short trips.** Mountain roads are slow. A drive that looks like 50 km can take over an hour.
3. **Start early.** Thunderstorms often build in the afternoon in July and August. Crowds and parking problems also peak from late morning.
4. **Lifts and huts have fixed seasons.** Many close in late September or early October, and some valleys nearly shut down before winter.
5. **No pass covers everything.** In 2026, the Dolomiti SuperSummer card did not cover the Seceda lifts, while the Gardena Card did.
6. **Carry some cash.** Most huts take cards now, but some still do not, and the signal for card machines can fail at altitude.
7. **Trailhead parking fills early** and often costs money. Know your plan B before you set off.
8. **Pack for four seasons.** It can be hot in the valley and cold, wet and windy on the ridge on the same day.
9. **Book hotels early for peak weeks.** Good places in July, August, Christmas and February often fill many months ahead.
10. **Public transport is useful but limited.** Many bus services run less often in the evening and on some days, so check the last return time.
11. **Rescue is not always free.** A helicopter rescue can be billed separately. Buy travel insurance that covers mountain rescue.
12. **Entry rules are changing.** The EU's ETIAS travel authorisation is expected to become mandatory for visa-exempt visitors, including Americans, from 2027. Check the current date before you fly.
