# 11. Val di Fassa, Catinaccio and Marmolada

Val di Fassa is the best-value base among the big Dolomites valleys. It sits between the Sella group, the Catinaccio (Rosengarten) and the Marmolada, the highest peak in the range. It is also the home of the Ladin people of Fassa, so you will hear Ladin spoken in the villages.

The valley is a good choice for walkers who want big scenery and lower prices, and for visitors who plan to use public transport, because guest cards here include free buses.

## Overview

| | |
|---|---|
| **Province** | Trentino (the Marmolada side is shared with Veneto) |
| **Main villages** | Canazei, Campitello, Alba, Pera di Fassa, Vigo di Fassa, Moena |
| **Language** | Italian and Ladin |
| **Best for** | Walkers, families, budget-minded visitors, public-transport users |
| **Car needed?** | Helpful, but the guest card makes buses practical |
| **Nearest airports** | Bolzano (about 1 to 1.5 hours), Verona, Venice, Innsbruck |
| **Season** | Summer lifts late May to mid-October; winter from December |

## Getting there and around

- **From Bolzano.** Bus line 180 (run by südtirolmobil) links Bolzano and Vigo di Fassa. By car, take the Karerpass road via the Bolzano Nord exit.
- **Val di Fassa Guest Card.** Ask your host for the free card. In 2026 it was valid for up to 20 consecutive days and gave free travel on Trentino Trasporti buses and regional trains, including the lines to the Pordoi, Sella, Fedaia, San Pellegrino and Costalunga passes. You validate each ride in the Mio Trentino app. Pass buses ran from June to September.
- **Lift pass.** The **Val di Fassa Panorama Pass** (30 May to 11 October 2026) cost €83 for 3 of 6 days, €116 for 6 of 6 days and €137 for 7 of 13 days (adult). Children under 8 travel free, and a junior pass can be free for families. The SuperSummer card also covers many lifts here (Chapter 4).

## 1. Catinaccio (Rosengarten) and the Vajolet Towers

| At a glance | |
|---|---|
| **Overview** | The Catinaccio group is a wall of pink-grey peaks above Val di Fassa. The six Vajolet Towers are its best-known landmark. |
| **Why visit** | One of the great walks of the Dolomites, with huts at each stage. |
| **Location and access** | Vajolet chairlift from Pera di Fassa to Pian Pecei (about 1,800 m), then a path to Gardeccia. Or the Ciampedie cable car from Vigo di Fassa. |
| **Visit time** | Gardeccia to the Vajolet and Preuss huts takes about 1 hour. To Rifugio Re Alberto (Gartlhütte) takes about 2 hours from Gardeccia. Allow a full day with the lift. |
| **Cost** | Lifts are covered by the Panorama Pass or sold as single tickets. Check current prices. |
| **Best time** | Summer and early September, in dry weather. Start early. |
| **Crowd reality** | Very busy in July and August, especially at Gardeccia. |
| **Difficulty** | Moderate. The track from Gardeccia up trail 546 is steep and rocky. |
| **Photography** | The towers catch late light. Walk beyond Gardeccia for quieter views. |
| **Practical tips** | Sturdy boots, layers, and cash for the huts. Check the opening dates of the huts you plan to use. |
| **Common mistakes** | Starting after 10:00. Wearing trainers on the rocky track. Not checking the last lift down. |
| **Nearby** | Lago di Carezza, Santner pass (a more demanding route), Rifugio Re Alberto. |
| **Drawbacks** | A long, steep day for beginners. |

> **Wish I'd Known:** The route over the Santner pass includes a via ferrata. It needs equipment and experience, so it is not a simple walk. If you are not sure, stay on the main track to the huts.

## 2. Marmolada

| At a glance | |
|---|---|
| **Overview** | At 3,343 m, the highest peak in the Dolomites. A cable car from Malga Ciapela climbs to Punta Rocca (3,265 m). |
| **Why visit** | The highest viewpoint in the Dolomites and a small Great War museum at the middle station. |
| **Location and access** | Malga Ciapela, in Veneto, just before the Passo Fedaia when approaching from the valley. Cable car in three sections via Serauta (2,950 m). |
| **Travel time** | The ride takes about 12 minutes. |
| **Cost (2026)** | Round trip €40 adult, €22 youth. The ticket includes the Great War museum at Serauta. |
| **Season (2026)** | 12 June to 27 September, daily, with the first departure at 09:00. |
| **Best time** | Clear days, early in the morning. |
| **Crowd reality** | Queues form in July and August. |
| **Difficulty** | Easy at the top station. The glacier and summit are not for independent visitors. |
| **Safety** | After the 2022 glacier collapse, summit access is only possible with a certified mountain guide in good conditions. |
| **Common mistakes** | Going up in poor weather. Wearing summer clothes. At 3,000 m, it can be cold even in July. |
| **Nearby** | Passo Fedaia and its lake, Passo Pordoi, Arabba. |
| **Drawbacks** | Weather dependent. Expensive for a short ride. |

## 3. Lago di Carezza (Karersee)

| At a glance | |
|---|---|
| **Overview** | A small lake at 1,520 m beneath the Latemar massif, known for its green-turquoise colour. |
| **Why visit** | A short stop on the road between Bolzano and Val di Fassa, and an easy walk for families. |
| **Cost (2026)** | No entry fee. Parking prices are reported differently: about €3 an hour, up to €30 a day, with no reservation (one source), and €5 to €7 for the first two hours with online reservation (another). Check the current rules. |
| **Visit time** | 30 to 60 minutes. A path goes around the lake, with a bar, restrooms and a shop. |
| **Best time** | Early morning, when the water is calm and the light is soft. |
| **Common mistakes** | Arriving at midday in August. Expecting a quiet spot. |
| **Drawbacks** | Short visit and busy car park. |

## 4. Col Rodella and the Sella side

- **Col Rodella.** A cable car from Campitello rises to a viewpoint over the Sassolungo and Sella. In 2026, it ran from 08:30 to 17:30 and carried non-skiers.
- **Sass Pordoi and Piz Boè.** Covered in Chapter 8.
- **Passo Fedaia.** A high pass road with a lake, beneath the Marmolada glacier.

## 5. The villages

- **Canazei.** The main town. Lifts, restaurants and shops. Good for a first stay.
- **Campitello.** Quieter than Canazei, with the Col Rodella cable car.
- **Pera and Vigo di Fassa.** Access to the Vajolet chairlift and Ciampedie.
- **Moena.** The southern gateway to the valley, quieter than Canazei, with a Ladin feel.

## Best walks in the area

| Walk | Level | Time | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gardeccia to Vajolet hut | Moderate | About 1 hour from Gardeccia | Steep rocky track |
| Gardeccia to Re Alberto | Moderate | About 2 hours from Gardeccia | Views of the towers |
| Lago di Carezza lake path | Easy | 30–60 minutes | Short loop |
| Piz Boè from Sass Pordoi | Moderate | 2–3.5 hours | Chapter 8 |

## Where to stay

**Canazei** or **Campitello** for lifts and buses. **Pera di Fassa** or **Vigo di Fassa** for the Catinaccio. **Moena** for a quieter stay. Fassa has good-value options in all three categories. See Chapter 6.

## Where to eat

Huts near the Vajolet and Pordoi serve traditional meals. In the valley, Ladin and Trentino dishes appear on many menus. Book dinner in peak weeks. Carry cash for huts.

## Photography notes

- Gardeccia and the Vajolet Towers in late afternoon.
- Carezza at first light.
- The Marmolada from Passo Fedaia in clear weather.
- For drones, check protected-area rules.

## Rainy-day options

Museums and spas in the valley villages, and a drive over the passes if the cloud lifts. The Great War museum at Serauta, on the Marmolada cable car, can be a useful stop on a cloudy day if the cable car is running.

## Common mistakes in this region

- Skipping the free guest card.
- Starting the Vajolet walk late.
- Taking the Santner pass route without experience.
- Visiting Carezza at midday.
- Going up the Marmolada in cloud.

## A simple plan for three days

- **Day 1:** Vajolet chairlift, Gardeccia and the huts. Backup: Col Rodella.
- **Day 2:** Marmolada cable car and Passo Fedaia early. Afternoon in Canazei.
- **Day 3:** Carezza at first light, then a bus or drive to Bolzano.
