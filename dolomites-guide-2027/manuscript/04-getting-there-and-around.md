# 4. Getting There and Getting Around

The Dolomites are easy to reach and slow to cross. You can fly into a major airport in a few hours, but the last stretch to your valley can take as long as the flight. This chapter helps you choose the right airport, decide whether you need a car, and avoid the costly transport mistakes.

## Choose your airport

No single airport is best. Pick the one that is closest to your base and has good flights from your home city.

| Airport | Best for | Typical journey to the Dolomites* |
|---|---|---|
| **Venice Marco Polo (VCE)** | Cortina, Alta Badia, Fassa, east Dolomites; many long-haul and low-cost flights | Cortina about 2 to 2.5 hours; Val Gardena about 3 hours |
| **Treviso (TSF)** | Budget flights from the UK and Europe; same side as Cortina | Cortina about 2 hours |
| **Verona (VRN)** | Val Gardena, Bolzano, Fassa; good for the west side | Bolzano about 1.5 to 2 hours |
| **Innsbruck (INN)** | A good alternative to Munich for the north side | Bolzano about 1.5 hours |
| **Munich (MUC)** | Many long-haul flights; direct trains to Bolzano | Bolzano about 3.5 to 4 hours |
| **Bolzano (BZO)** | Nearest to Val Gardena; limited flights | Val Gardena about 45 minutes |
| **Milan (MXP, LIN, BGY)** | Cheap flights; long transfer | Bolzano about 4 hours or more |

*Approximate, in normal traffic, by road. Add time at weekends in summer and winter. Check a route planner for your dates.

**About Bolzano airport.** In 2026, the airline SkyAlps flew to Bolzano from several German and Italian cities, including Berlin, Düsseldorf and Hamburg. Winter 2026/27 flights were scheduled almost daily from those three. Schedules change often, so check the latest timetable. When the flight fits, it is the quickest way to Val Gardena.

> **Wish I'd Known:** Cheap flights to Milan or Venice can look like bargains until you add a three-to-four-hour transfer. For a short trip, an airport closer to your base often costs less overall.

## By train

Trains are a good option for the main valleys, but they do not reach most mountain villages.

- **Brenner line.** Trains run from Munich through Innsbruck to Bolzano and on to Verona. In 2026, a typical Munich to Bolzano trip took about 3 hours 54 minutes, with around seven direct trains a day.
- **From Verona.** Trains to Bolzano take about two hours.
- **From Venice.** Expect about four hours, with a change.
- **Valley line.** The Pusteria (Val Pusteria) railway runs from Fortezza/Franzensfeste through Brunico to Dobbiaco and San Candido. It is useful for Braies, Sesto and the Tre Cime area.
- **No trains to Cortina, Val Gardena or Alta Badia.** You continue by bus.

**Tip:** Buy long-distance tickets early. Regional trains within South Tyrol are covered by the Südtirol Mobilcard (see below). Long-distance trains such as EuroCity, Railjet and Frecce are not.

## By airport bus and shuttle

- **Venice to Cortina.** The Cortina Express runs a direct bus. In 2026 it took about 2 hours 10 minutes to under 2.5 hours, with fares around €21 one way (check the current price).
- **Bolzano to Val Gardena.** SAD bus 350 runs from Bolzano into Val Gardena. From Bolzano airport, a short shuttle or bus link connects to the main bus station. The combined trip from the airport to Val Gardena was reported at about €7–8 and around 1.5 hours (check current figures).
- **Private shuttles and taxis.** Shared and private airport transfers are widely available. They cost more but save changes, especially with luggage. Book ahead in peak weeks.

## Driving

A car gives freedom, but it also means parking fees, restricted roads and slow mountain traffic.

**Main routes**
- **A22 Brenner motorway** (Autostrada del Brennero). This is the main north–south route. Exit at **Chiusa/Klausen** for Val Gardena, **Bressanone/Brixen** for Val Pusteria and Alta Badia (via Brunico), and **Bolzano Nord** for the Karerpass and Val di Fassa.
- **Venice to Cortina.** Take the motorway to the Belluno area, then the main road north (SS51) to Cortina.
- **From Austria.** You need an Austrian motorway vignette, and the Brenner A13 has an extra toll of about €12.50 per trip. Check current prices. (The 2025 vignette ran from about €8.60 for one day.)

**Italian road rules that catch visitors**
- **Tolls.** Italian motorways charge by distance. Take a ticket on entry and pay on exit.
- **Speed limits.** Motorways 130 km/h; main roads outside towns 110 km/h; secondary roads 90 km/h; towns 50 km/h unless signed otherwise. Obey the signs.
- **ZTL (limited traffic zones).** Many town centres restrict cars. Entering without permission can lead to a fine, usually about €80–330, and the notice may arrive months later. Rental firms pass on your details. Look for signs showing a red circle and a *ZTL* panel. If your hotel is inside a ZTL, ask it to register your plate.
- **Parking colours.** Blue lines are paid parking. White lines are free. Yellow lines are reserved. Always check the nearest sign.
- **Fuel.** In August 2026, the national average self-service price was about €2.00 per litre for petrol and €2.12 for diesel. Self-service is cheaper than full service. Fill up in the valley before long mountain drives.

**Mountain pass driving**
- Roads are narrow, with hairpin bends, cyclists and motorbikes. Allow double the time your map app suggests on weekends in July and August.
- On narrow sections, vehicles going uphill usually have priority. Be ready to reverse to a wider spot.
- Use a lower gear on long descents, so your brakes do not overheat.
- Check for road closures on bike days (Chapter 3).

**Winter driving**
- Winter tyres or snow chains are required on many roads. On the Brenner motorway and in the municipality of Bolzano, winter equipment is compulsory from 15 November to 15 April. Elsewhere in South Tyrol, the rule applies in winter conditions.
- Rental cars in winter normally come with winter tyres. Confirm this when you book.
- Carry chains if you plan to drive high passes in snow.

### Do you need a rental car?

| Your plan | Car? |
|---|---|
| One base in Val Gardena, Alta Badia or Fassa, using lifts and buses | Probably not |
| Two or more regions in one trip | Yes, for flexibility |
| Early starts for sunrise or to beat crowds | Yes |
| Staying in Bolzano or Brixen | No |
| Hut-to-hut trek | No |
| Winter ski trip | No, if your hotel is near the lifts |

**Rental tips**
- Book 3 to 6 months ahead for summer. Automatics sell out first and cost about 20–30% more than manuals.
- Choose a small car. Narrow roads and tight parking favour compact models.
- Check the insurance excess and what is covered on unpaved or toll roads.
- Many non-EU drivers need an International Driving Permit. Confirm with the rental firm.
- Photograph the car before you leave the lot.

## Public transport inside the Dolomites

Buses link most valleys, passes and lift stations. They are the best way to avoid parking stress.

- **Südtirol Mobilcard.** One card covers regional trains and buses in South Tyrol. The price in 2026 was about €20–22 for one day, €30–32 for three days and €50–52 for seven days (two sources gave slightly different figures; check the official site). It excludes long-distance trains and some night buses, but includes certain cable cars such as the Ritten line.
- **Südtirol Guest Pass.** Many hotels in South Tyrol give guests free regional transport. Ask when you book.
- **Veneto and Trentino.** Different systems apply. Check the local tourist office for the area where you stay.
- **Timetables.** Services are less frequent in the evening and at times in the off-season. Screenshot the last bus back before you set out.

## Lift passes and summer cards

Lift costs are a big part of a Dolomites budget. Pick a card based on where you will actually ride.

| Card | 2026 price (adult) | Covers | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dolomiti SuperSummer** (14 May – 8 Nov 2026) | €67 (1 day); €145 (3 of 4 days); €185 (5 of 7 days) | Over 100 lifts across about 12 areas. **Not Seceda in 2026.** | Multi-valley trips |
| **Gardena Card** (6 Jun – 11 Oct 2026) | €124 (3 days); €160 (6 days) | 17 lifts in Val Gardena, **including Seceda** | Val Gardena stays |
| **Val di Fassa Panorama Pass** (30 May – 11 Oct 2026) | €83 (3 of 6 days); €116 (6 of 6); €137 (7 of 13) | 31 lifts in Fassa, including Vajolet lifts | Fassa stays |
| **Points Card** | €100 for 1,000 points | Lifts on a points basis; **not valid in Val Gardena** | Light users, family sharing |
| **Alta Badia Summer Card; 3 Zinnen Mountain Card** | Check each site | Local lifts | Local stays |

**How to choose.** Count the lifts you will ride and add up single fares. If the card costs less, buy it. If you will only ride one or two lifts, buy single tickets.

## Car-free Dolomites: how well does it work?

| Base | Car-free rating | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Bolzano, Brixen** | Excellent | Trains and city buses; day trips by bus |
| **Ortisei, Santa Cristina, Selva** | Good | Buses and lifts connect to Seceda, Alpe di Siusi, Sella Ronda by bus |
| **Alta Badia (Corvara area)** | Good in season | Buses and lifts; check evening buses |
| **Val Pusteria (Dobbiaco, San Candido)** | Good | Valley train; bus to Braies, Tre Cime shuttle |
| **Cortina** | Fair | No train; good town buses, fewer cross-valley options |
| **Fassa (Canazei)** | Fair to good | Local buses; fewer links outside the valley |

*These ratings are the author's judgement. Check local bus links for your own dates.*

**Car-free tips.** Base yourself in a valley with good buses, buy a transport card, plan day trips around bus times, and use taxis for the early-morning start that matters most.

## Money-saving transport strategies

1. Take a shuttle instead of a rental car if you will stay in one valley.
2. Ask your hotel for a guest pass.
3. Split rental costs among friends.
4. Book the cheapest airport that fits your base, then count transfer costs.
5. Avoid driving up to famous places in peak hours when buses are available. You may save parking fees and stress.

## Common mistakes

- Renting a car and then paying for parking you do not use.
- Choosing a base without checking bus links.
- Not allowing time for slow roads.
- Entering a ZTL by accident.
- Assuming the lift card works in every valley.
