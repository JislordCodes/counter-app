# 8. Alta Badia, Sella Ronda and the Great Passes

Alta Badia (Hochabtei) is the heart of the Ladin Dolomites. Its villages sit near the Sella massif and the roads that wind over four of the best-known mountain passes. It is a good base for drivers and cyclists, for food lovers, and for anyone who wants easy access to several valleys.

This chapter covers Alta Badia, the **Sella Ronda** (the loop around the Sella massif), the high passes, and the hike and via ferrata most visitors ask about: Piz Boè and Lago Pisciadù.

## Overview

| | |
|---|---|
| **Province** | South Tyrol (Passo Pordoi and Fassa side are in Trentino; Arabba is in Veneto) |
| **Main villages** | Corvara, Colfosco, La Villa, San Cassiano, Badia (Abtei), San Martino in Badia |
| **Language** | Ladin, German, Italian |
| **Best for** | Drivers, cyclists, food lovers, skiers, families who want quiet villages |
| **Car needed?** | Helpful. Buses and lifts serve the main villages in season. |

## Getting there and around

- **By road.** From the A22, exit at Bressanone/Brixen and follow Val Pusteria to Brunico, then turn south into Val Badia. From Val Gardena, cross the **Passo Gardena**.
- **By bus.** Passo Falzarego is reachable by bus from Colfosco, Corvara, La Villa and San Cassiano (2026 information). Ask your hotel about a guest pass for local buses.
- **Lifts.** A local summer card for Alta Badia exists. The SuperSummer card (Chapter 4) also covers many lifts across the region.

## The four great passes

The Sella Ronda links four passes around the Sella massif. They are among the most famous mountain roads in Europe.

| Pass | Height | Connects | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Passo Gardena** (Grödner Joch) | 2,121 m | Val Gardena and Alta Badia | Start for the Pisciadù walk and the Brigata Tridentina via ferrata |
| **Passo Sella** | about 2,200 m | Val Gardena and Val di Fassa | Sassolungo below the road (Chapter 7) |
| **Passo Pordoi** | about 2,239 m | Fassa and Arabba | Start for the Sass Pordoi cable car and Piz Boè |
| **Passo Campolongo** | about 1,875 m | Corvara and Arabba | The lowest and easiest of the four |

*Heights are rounded. Check the sign at each pass.*

## 1. The Sella Ronda by car

| At a glance | |
|---|---|
| **Overview** | A loop of about 49 km around the Sella massif over four passes. |
| **Why visit** | The easiest way to see four passes and three valleys in one day. |
| **Travel time** | About 3 hours for one lap with a coffee stop (2026 estimate). In busy periods, allow more. |
| **Visit time** | A full day if you stop at viewpoints and huts. |
| **Cost** | Fuel and parking only. Parking at the passes can be paid. |
| **Best time** | Early morning on a weekday. Midday on summer weekends is slow. |
| **Crowd reality** | Heavy traffic from cars, motorbikes, coaches and cyclists. |
| **Difficulty** | Easy driving but many hairpin bends. |
| **Photography** | Pull over only in marked spaces. Early and late light work best. |
| **Practical tips** | Fuel up in the valley. Check the weather. Have a car with good brakes and use a low gear on descents. |
| **Common mistakes** | Starting at 11:00. Planning too many stops. Driving on Sellaronda Bike Day. |
| **Nearby** | Sass Pordoi cable car, Lagazuoi, Passo Falzarego. |
| **Drawbacks** | Traffic, noise and limited parking at popular spots. |

**Direction.** The loop can be driven either way. Use your map app to check traffic before you choose.

> **Wish I'd Known:** On **Sellaronda Bike Day**, Sella, Gardena, Pordoi and Campolongo were closed to cars and motorbikes from 08:30 to 16:00 in 2026. The first date for 2027 is reported as **5 June**. A second date in September is likely, but not confirmed. A planned traffic limit on the Passo Gardena was dropped in 2026 in favour of a digital test phase, so check the current rules for 2027.

## 2. The Sella Ronda by bike

- **Distance:** the mountain-bike route is about 57 to 60 km, with about 1,000 m of climbing. The road route used on Bike Day is about 53 km with about 1,600 m of climbing.
- **Time:** 6 to 9 hours on a mountain bike or e-bike, including a lunch stop. Guided tours using lifts were about 6 to 7 hours.
- **Who it suits:** fit cyclists, and e-bike riders of moderate fitness.
- **Best day:** Sellaronda Bike Day (the roads are closed to motor traffic), or a weekday morning.
- **Common mistakes:** Starting late, not packing rain gear, and underestimating the climbs.

## 3. Piz Boè from Passo Pordoi

| At a glance | |
|---|---|
| **Overview** | At 3,152 m, Piz Boè is the highest summit in the Sella group and one of the easiest 3,000-metre peaks in the Dolomites. |
| **Why visit** | A real high-mountain summit without a technical climb, reached by cable car. |
| **Location and access** | Cable car from Passo Pordoi to Sass Pordoi (2,950 m). |
| **Cost (2026)** | About €30 to €32 round trip. Season mid-May to early November, about 09:00 to 17:00. |
| **Visit time** | 2 to 3.5 hours for the 5.3 km round trip from the cable car, plus lift time. |
| **Best time** | Summer and early September, in clear weather and early in the day. |
| **Crowd reality** | Busy at midday. Parking at Passo Pordoi is difficult later in the day. |
| **Difficulty** | Moderate. About 430 m of climbing. Loose gravel and some rock scrambling with chains and iron rungs near the top. |
| **Photography** | Wide views over the Dolomites on clear days. |
| **Practical tips** | Good boots. Sun protection, because the trail is open and exposed. Rifugio Forcella Pordoi (about 20 June to 30 September) and the summit hut both serve food. |
| **Common mistakes** | Wearing trainers on loose gravel. Forgetting the climb back to the cable car. Going up in cloud or with storms forecast. |
| **Nearby** | Passo Pordoi, Arabba, Val di Fassa. |
| **Drawbacks** | High altitude. Weather can turn quickly. |

## 4. Lago Pisciadù and the Brigata Tridentina via ferrata

| At a glance | |
|---|---|
| **Overview** | A small lake at 2,564 m with a hut above it (Rifugio Cavazza, 2,585 m), reached from Passo Gardena on foot or by via ferrata. |
| **Why visit** | A high-mountain lake and one of the best-known via ferrata routes in the Dolomites. |
| **Location and access** | Starts at the Passo Gardena road, around 1,956 m on the Val Badia side. |
| **Via ferrata facts** | Medium difficulty, about 700 m of ascent and 300 m of ferrata, about 3 hours. It includes ladders and a suspension bridge. Best July to September. |
| **Hut season** | Rifugio Cavazza about 21 June to 20 September. |
| **Difficulty** | Walking route: moderate. Via ferrata: requires equipment and experience. |
| **Practical tips** | Hire a guide if you have not done a via ferrata before (Chapter 14). Carry helmet, harness and a lanyard with energy absorber. |
| **Common mistakes** | Attempting the ferrata without equipment. Starting late. Ignoring afternoon storms. |
| **Drawbacks** | Exposed, and can be busy in peak weeks. |

## 5. Alta Badia villages and Ladin culture

- **Corvara, Colfosco, La Villa, San Cassiano.** The main bases. Each has hotels, restaurants and lifts. San Cassiano is quieter and known for fine dining.
- **Museum Ladin Ciastel de Tor (San Martino in Badia).** About the culture and history of the Ladin people. Summer hours in 2026 were Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00 to 18:00, closed Monday. A second site in San Cassiano, the Ursus ladinicus museum, covers the cave bear and the geology of the Dolomites.
- **Santa Croce (Heilig Kreuz).** A pilgrimage church at the foot of the mountain of the same name, reached on foot. Ask the tourist office for the current route and timing.
- **Michelin-starred dining.** In the 2026 guide, two Alta Badia restaurants held a star: La Stüa de Michil (Corvara) and Porcino (Badia), which was new in 2026. Book well ahead. See Chapter 16.

## 6. Passo Falzarego and Lagazuoi

The Lagazuoi cable car leaves from Passo Falzarego (about 2,105 m) and was priced at €29.50 in 2026. The Great War tunnels and the Cinque Torri are covered in Chapter 9. Buses from Alta Badia villages reach Falzarego.

## Best walks and routes in the area

| Walk or route | Level | Time | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Santa Croce walk | Easy to moderate | Check locally | Church and meadows |
| Piz Boè from Sass Pordoi | Moderate | 2–3.5 hours | Cable car needed |
| Lago Pisciadù | Moderate | Several hours | Via ferrata option |
| Alta Badia High Route | Hard | About 7 days | Circular, for expert hikers |

## Where to stay

Choose **Corvara** or **La Villa** for lifts and passes, **Colfosco** for a quieter base near Gardena, and **San Cassiano** for gourmet dining and Falzarego. See Chapter 6.

## Photography notes

- Early light on the Sella and the passes is usually best.
- Roads are narrow. Park only in marked spaces.
- Cyclists are everywhere. Take care when stopping.
- For drones, check nature park rules first.

## Rainy-day options

The Ladin museums in San Martino and San Cassiano, spa hotels and cafés in the villages. If the cloud lifts, a low walk to Santa Croce.

## Common mistakes in this region

- Planning the Sella Ronda for Sellaronda Bike Day.
- Starting a pass drive after 11:00 in summer.
- Doing Piz Boè in poor weather.
- Trying a via ferrata without equipment or experience.
- Booking only one night in Alta Badia and missing the quiet evenings.

## A simple plan for two days

- **Day 1:** Early start for the Sella Ronda by car or bus, with a stop at Passo Pordoi and the Sass Pordoi cable car if the weather is clear.
- **Day 2:** Santa Croce walk or the Ladin museum, and a long lunch. Backup plan: a spa or the museums.
