# 6. Where to Stay: Choosing the Right Base

Your base decides how much of your trip you spend walking, and how much you spend driving. A good base puts your main lifts and trailheads within a short walk or bus ride. A bad one adds an hour of driving to every day.

This chapter explains how to choose, compares the main bases, and tells you what to expect from each type of accommodation.

## Three questions that decide it

1. **What will you do most days?** Walking from lifts, driving passes, cycling, skiing or city sightseeing?
2. **Will you have a car?** If not, you need a village with good buses and lifts nearby.
3. **How many places do you want to see?** More than two regions usually means two bases, not one.

## Bases compared

| Base | Best for | Car needed? | Price level | Main drawback |

## The bases, one by one

### Ortisei, Santa Cristina and Selva (Val Gardena)

- **Why choose it.** This is the easiest base for first-time visitors. Lifts, restaurants and buses are all within walking distance. Seceda starts in Ortisei. The Alpe di Siusi is within reach by lift or bus (check current links). Selva is close to Sassolungo and the Sella passes.
- **Who it suits.** First-timers, couples, families, skiers and car-free visitors.
- **Parking.** Many hotels include a space. Public parking in Ortisei is limited and fills early in peak weeks.
- **Drawbacks.** Busy in July, August and winter holidays. Prices are above average.

### Alpe di Siusi (Compatsch, Siusi, Castelrotto)

- **Why choose it.** Sleeping on or near the meadows gives you quiet mornings and evenings, before day visitors arrive and after they leave.
- **Who it suits.** Families, gentle walkers, photographers and couples.
- **Access.** Hotel guests on the meadows get road permits from their host. If you stay in Siusi or Castelrotto, you can use the cable car or bus.
- **Drawbacks.** Fewer evening options. Fewer bus links to other valleys. Rules on cars (Chapter 3).

### Alta Badia: Corvara, Colfosco, La Villa, San Cassiano

- **Why choose it.** It sits at the crossroads of the Sella Ronda, the Gardena, Campolongo and Falzarego roads. It is also known for fine dining.
- **Who it suits.** Cyclists, drivers, food lovers, and skiers.
- **Car need.** Helpful, but buses and lifts work well in season.
- **Drawbacks.** Fewer budget options. Many places close between seasons.

### Cortina d'Ampezzo

- **Why choose it.** It is the best base for Cinque Torri, Lagazuoi, Passo Giau and Lago di Sorapis. The town is livelier and larger than most Dolomites villages.
- **Who it suits.** Drivers, history and Great War fans, and people who like a proper town.
- **Car need.** Helpful. There is no railway.
- **Parking.** Limited and often paid. Check whether your hotel includes it.
- **Drawbacks.** Prices rise in August. The area is far from Val Gardena (allow about two hours or more by road).

### Sesto, Dobbiaco and San Candido (Val Pusteria / Alta Pusteria)

- **Why choose it.** Best for the Tre Cime, Val Fiscalina and Lago di Braies. It has a valley railway and is cheaper than the big resorts.
- **Who it suits.** Walkers, rail travellers, budget-minded visitors.
- **Car need.** Not essential.
- **Drawbacks.** Further from the Sella passes.

### Val di Fassa: Canazei, Campitello, Moena, Vigo di Fassa

- **Why choose it.** Good value and access to the Catinaccio/Rosengarten, Marmolada and the Pordoi and Sella passes.
- **Who it suits.** Families, hikers on a budget, and visitors who want a more local feel.
- **Car need.** Helpful.
- **Drawbacks.** Fewer high-end hotels.

### Bolzano and Brixen (cities)

- **Why choose it.** Trains, buses, museums and wine. Good if your trip includes rainy days or low season. Bolzano is home to the museum of the Iceman, Ötzi.
- **Who it suits.** Car-free visitors, culture lovers, and early- or late-season travellers.
- **Drawbacks.** Bolzano is hot in summer and you must travel 45 minutes or more to reach high mountain scenery.

### Brunico and Plan de Corones

- **Why choose it.** It is a good base for Plan de Corones, the Messner Mountain Museum and cycling. It is also within reach of Alta Badia.
- **Who it suits.** Cyclists, culture fans and winter skiers.

## Types of accommodation

| Type | What it is | Typical use | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hotel with half board** | Breakfast and dinner included | Couples, families | Common in the mountains; drinks extra |
| **Garni or B&B** | Breakfast only | Flexible travellers | Good if you want to eat out |
| **Apartment** | Self-catering | Families, longer stays | Check minimum stays and cleaning fees |
| **Farm stay** (*Urlaub auf dem Bauernhof*, *agriturismo*) | Working farm with rooms or flats | Families, couples | South Tyrol has a recognised farm-stay brand called Roter Hahn (Red Rooster). Ideal for children |
| **Mountain hut (rifugio)** | Dorm or private room, half board | Trekkers, early starts | Book months ahead; see Chapter 13 |
| **Camping** | Campsites in the valleys | Budget and van travellers | Wild camping is not allowed in most areas |

**Minimum stays.** Some hotels and apartments require minimum stays or fixed arrival days in peak weeks. Ask before you book.

## Seasonal pricing and booking windows

- **Highest prices:** mid-July to August, Christmas and New Year, February.
- **Lower prices:** late June, September, and most of April, May and November (although many places are closed).
- **Book early for peak weeks.** Aim for 6 to 12 months ahead for July, August, Christmas and February (Chapter 3).
- **Check cancellation terms.** Choose a flexible rate if the weather or booking rules could change your plans.

## Picks by traveller type

| If you are... | Look in... | Look for... |
|---|---|---|
| **On a budget** | Val Pusteria, Fassa, farm stays | Guesthouses, apartments, farm stays; a free guest pass |
| **Mid-range** | Val Gardena, Alta Badia, Cortina | 3- or 4-star hotel with half board near a lift |
| **Luxury** | Alta Badia, Ortisei, Cortina | 4- or 5-star spa hotel; book far ahead |
| **A family** | Alpe di Siusi, Val Gardena, Fassa | Apartments or farm stays with a playground; flat walks nearby |
| **A couple** | Ortisei, San Cassiano, Alpe di Siusi | Quiet room with a view and half board |
| **Without a car** | Bolzano, Brixen, Ortisei, Dobbiaco | Hotel close to a bus stop or lift; guest pass |

## Two-base strategies

Two bases of three or four nights each often give a better trip than one base for a week.

- **Val Gardena + Cortina.** Covers Seceda, Alpe di Siusi and Sella Ronda, then Cinque Torri, Lagazuoi and Giau.
- **Alta Badia + Val Pusteria.** Covers the passes and the Tre Cime and Braies.
- **Val di Fassa + Val Gardena.** Covers Catinaccio and Marmolada, then Seceda and Alpe di Siusi.
- **Bolzano + any mountain base.** Gives you a city stay for culture or bad weather.

> **Wish I'd Known:** The drive between two bases often takes one and a half to two hours or more on mountain roads, and it can feel longer on a hot weekend. Treat the move as a half-day, with no big walk planned around it.

## Common mistakes

- Choosing the cheapest room in a village with no bus links.
- Staying far from lifts in peak season and relying on parking.
- Booking half board and then eating out.
- Using three bases in one week.
- Ignoring minimum-stay rules.
