# PART 4: LOCAL LIFE

# 16. Food and Drink

Food is one of the best reasons to take your time in the Dolomites. Meals here mix Italian, Austrian and Ladin traditions, and a long lunch at a mountain hut is part of the day, not a break from it.

This chapter explains what to eat, how huts and restaurants work, what meals cost, and how to eat well on any budget.

## What to eat

### Dishes you will see everywhere

| Dish | What it is | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Canederli** (Knödel) | Large dumplings made from bread, milk and eggs, often with speck or cheese. Served in broth or with butter and cheese. | A plate was about €8 at a festival in 2026. Good for lunch. |
| **Schlutzkrapfen** | Half-moon ravioli filled with spinach and ricotta, served with melted butter and cheese. | Common in South Tyrol. A good vegetarian choice. |
| **Speck** | Smoked, cured ham. Speck Alto Adige has a protected origin label (IGP). | Served as a board with bread, or in dumplings. |
| **Gulasch and Wurst** | Goulash and sausages, often with sauerkraut. | Hearty hut food. |
| **Kaiserschmarrn** | Shredded sweet pancake with fruit compote. | A classic hut dessert. |
| **Apple strudel** | Pastry filled with apple, raisins and cinnamon. | Served warm, often with cream. |
| **Polenta** | Cornmeal, served with cheese, mushrooms or meat. | Common on the Veneto and Trentino sides. |

### Ladin dishes

The Ladin valleys (Val Gardena, Val Badia, Val di Fassa, Livinallongo and Cortina) have their own dishes, based on simple farm ingredients.

- **Panicia:** a barley soup.
- **Tutres:** fried pastries filled with spinach or cabbage.
- **Cajincí:** ravioli filled with spinach and cottage cheese, with melted butter.
- **Furtaies:** sweet pastry whorls.
- **Wild game:** dishes made with game play an important part in Ladin cooking.

### Cheese and local products

- **Puzzone di Moena** is a washed-rind cheese from Val di Fassa with protected status (PDO since 2014). It has a strong smell. Try a small piece first.
- **Casunziei** are beetroot-filled ravioli associated with Cortina.

### Törggelen: the autumn tradition

In autumn, farm inns serve a traditional meal with new wine and roasted chestnuts.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Season** | Early October to mid-November (some sources say late November) |
| **What you eat** | Schlutzkrapfen, dumplings, sauerkraut, salted meats (Surfleisch), sausages, doughnuts and roast chestnuts |
| **What you drink** | *Suser*, the cloudy new wine |
| **Cost (2026 examples)** | About €36 to €39 per person, excluding drinks. A vegetarian option was €32 at one place. |
| **Booking** | Required in many places. Some need groups of six or more, and some open only Thursday to Sunday evenings. |
| **Best for** | Couples, friends and families who like a long, traditional evening |

## Drinks

### Wine

South Tyrol is a serious wine region.

- **Lagrein.** A full-bodied, deep red wine from around Bolzano.
- **Schiava (Vernatsch).** A light, easy red.
- **St. Magdalener.** A traditional red blend of Vernatsch and Lagrein.
- **Gewürztraminer.** An aromatic white wine named after the village of Termeno (Tramin).
- **Pinot Blanc, Sauvignon, Riesling and Kerner.** Fresh whites, especially around Merano.

If you do not know what to choose, ask for the house wine (*vino della casa*) or a local wine by the glass.

### Coffee, beer and spirits

- **Coffee.** At the bar, an espresso costs about €1 to €1.50. Bolzano had the highest average espresso price in Italy in 2026, at about €1.47. A cappuccino averaged about €2.18 in Bolzano. Sitting down costs more than standing.
- **Spritz.** A popular afternoon drink.
- **Schnapps.** Offered after meals in many huts.
- **Water.** Tap water is safe to drink in the valleys.

## How mountain huts work for food

| Topic | What to know |
|---|---|
| **Service** | Most huts have table service. Sit down, read the menu, and pay at the end. |
| **Hours** | Hut kitchens often run about midday to mid-afternoon. Some huts open from morning to evening, others close earlier. Check before you arrive. |
| **Menu** | Starters, mains and desserts are priced separately. Drinks are extra. |
| **Prices** | A set lunch at one high hut was about €40 per person in 2026. Dinner at huts ranged from about €30 to €120 depending on the hut. |
| **Payment** | Most take cards, but some do not. Carry cash. |
| **Busy times** | Midday, especially at huts on the Tre Cime loop, near Seceda and at Cinque Torri. |
| **Overnight guests** | Half board includes a set dinner and breakfast. Tell the hut about allergies when you book. |

> **Wish I'd Known:** At the busiest huts in July and August, tables fill around midday and you may have to wait. Arrive before 12:00, or eat later in the afternoon. If you want to reserve, ask the hut a day or two ahead.

## Restaurants and what to expect

### Typical costs (2026 estimates)

| Meal | Cost per person |
|---|---|
| Typical lunch | About €20 |
| Typical dinner | About €40 |
| Hut set lunch (high mountain) | About €40 |
| Fine dining | Much higher; check the restaurant's menu |

### Tipping and the coperto

- **Coperto.** A small charge per person for the table setting and bread, usually €1 to €3. It is legal and printed on the menu. It is not a tip.
- **Servizio.** If a service charge appears on the bill, do not tip as well.
- **Tip.** If neither is on the bill, 5 to 10 percent is generous. Rounding up is also fine.
- **At the bar.** No tip is expected.

### When and where to book

- **Reserve dinner** in July, August, December and February. Book fine dining months ahead.
- **Kitchen hours.** Many restaurants serve lunch from about 12:00 to 14:00 and dinner from about 18:30 or 19:00. Closing days vary, and many places close one day a week, often Monday. Check before you go.
- **Between seasons.** In April, May and November, many places are closed.

## Dietary needs

- **Vegetarian.** Schlutzkrapfen, cheese dumplings, polenta, salads and strudel are common. Many menus have a vegetarian option, but check that soups and dumplings are not made with meat stock or bacon.
- **Gluten-free.** Italy has a strong awareness of coeliac disease. In 2026, huts such as Carlo Valentini, Sasso Piatto, Pralongià and Ütia Bioch listed gluten-free dishes. At Rifugio Fedaia, guests are asked to mention needs when booking. In Cortina, Al Passetto was certified by the Italian Coeliac Society.
- **Allergies.** Tell the staff in the local language. In German: *Ich habe eine Allergie gegen...*. In Italian: *Sono allergico a...*.
- **Vegan.** Possible with notice, but menus are cheese- and butter-heavy. Ask ahead.

> **Tip:** Contact a hut or restaurant when you book to confirm dietary options. Do not rely on menus online.

## Eating well on a budget

1. **Make lunch your main meal.** Set menus are often cheaper than dinner.
2. **Buy picnic supplies** in valley supermarkets before you take the lift.
3. **Share dishes** at huts. Portions can be large.
4. **Drink water** from fountains in villages (where marked as drinking water).
5. **Use bars for breakfast.** Standing at the counter is cheaper than a table.
6. **Avoid tourist traps** at lift stations and the most famous viewpoints. Prices are higher there.

## Common mistakes

- Arriving at a hut at 12:30 in August and finding no table.
- Not carrying cash.
- Ordering dishes without asking what is in them.
- Booking a half-board hotel and eating out every night.
- Missing Törggelen because it was not booked.

## What to do next

1. Choose one food experience to book early: a Törggelen evening, a special dinner or a hut lunch.
2. Check closing days for the places you want.
