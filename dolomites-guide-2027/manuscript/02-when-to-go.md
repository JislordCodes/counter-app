# When to Go

The Dolomites have two main seasons, a summer hiking season and a winter ski season. Between them lie two quiet "shoulder" periods when many lifts and huts close. The right time to go depends mainly on what you want to do.

## Summer: late June to mid-September

This is the classic season for walking. Cable cars and chairlifts are running, mountain huts (*rifugi*) are open, and the high trails are usually free of snow. It is also the busiest and most expensive period.

- **Late June to early July.** Meadows are in flower and days are very long. Some high routes can still hold patches of old snow, especially on north-facing slopes. Huts and lifts are usually open, but a few start a little later in the season.
- **July and August.** Warmest weather, but also the largest crowds and the highest prices. Italian school holidays peak around the 15th of August, which is the public holiday of *Ferragosto*. Afternoon thunderstorms are most frequent now, so start walks early.
- **Early to mid-September.** Many experienced visitors consider this the best time. The weather is often stable, the air is clear, crowds thin out and prices ease. Huts begin to close in the second half of the month, so check dates for any hut you plan to stay in.

## Autumn: late September to October

The larch trees turn gold, usually from the middle of October at mid elevations, and the light is wonderful. Many lifts and most high huts close around the end of September or the start of October, though dates vary by area. Some valleys effectively shut for a few weeks before the winter season. If you want autumn colour, plan for lower-level walks and check which hotels and restaurants are open.

## Winter: December to March

The ski season normally begins in late November or early December and runs until around Easter at the larger resorts. The big draw is the **Dolomiti Superski** network, which links twelve ski areas and about 1,200 kilometres of pistes on a single lift pass. Chapter 10 covers winter in detail. Christmas markets in Bolzano, Bressanone, Merano and Ortisei also draw visitors in December.

Peak weeks are Christmas and New Year, February half-term and the weeks around Carnival, and Easter. Outside these, you often find calmer slopes and lower prices.

## Spring: April to mid-June

Spring is the quietest time. Snow lingers at altitude, many lifts have closed for maintenance, and many hotels shut for a break. Valley walking, cities and wine villages work well, and the apple orchards of South Tyrol blossom in April. Higher mountain roads open gradually, and the opening dates depend on snow clearance.

## What about 2027?

Dates are set season by season, so check the official sites in spring 2027. Look at the following:

- Opening and closing dates for the lifts and cable cars you want to use.
- Opening dates for any hut you want to stay in.
- Opening dates for high mountain passes. Many close for winter and reopen when cleared.
- The date when reservations open for popular sights.

## Quick guide by interest

- **Easy walks, flowers and long days:** late June to mid-July.
- **Stable weather, thinner crowds:** early to mid-September.
- **Autumn colour and quiet villages:** October, with lower-level walks.
- **Skiing:** January to March for the most reliable snow cover.
- **Via ferrata:** July to mid-September, when routes are clear of snow.
- **Road cycling:** June to September, with pass roads usually fully clear by mid-June.
- **Cheapest prices:** shoulder seasons, and mid-week in September.
